Our story

More than a cup of juice.

Based in Orlando, Florida, The Daily Squeeze plans to work with local grocery stores to collect fruits and vegetables that are still safe to eat but might otherwise be discarded. We want to turn that produce into fresh drinks while supporting people who face barriers to employment.

Our mission brings together two goals: reducing unnecessary food waste and creating meaningful opportunities in our community. Through a mobile juice cart, we hope to make fresh drinks accessible at local events and community spaces.

What we offer

Fresh drinks with a purpose.

Our proposed products are built around fresh ingredients, convenience and creative use of surplus produce. Flavors will change based on the safe fruit available.

MARKETING

Introducing The Daily Squeeze: A Fresh Approach to Juice

By Adam Redding · Marketing / CEO

The Daily Squeeze is developing a fresh, environmentally responsible way for customers to enjoy juice. Our concept is simple: use safe fruit that is nearing the end of its retail shelf life instead of allowing it to go to waste. By turning that produce into drinks, we aim to offer customers something enjoyable while addressing a problem that affects grocery stores and communities.

Our products will focus on fresh ingredients and a variety of flavors. We plan to work with local stores and suppliers whenever possible, helping us build community relationships while giving surplus fruit another purpose. Our mobile format is intended to make fresh drinks convenient for customers at different locations.

What makes The Daily Squeeze different is the purpose behind each purchase. Along with reducing food waste, we want to provide employment opportunities for people who have been overlooked, helping them gain experience and build toward future careers. This social mission gives our brand a meaningful story to share with customers and potential community partners.

For potential investors, our marketing opportunity is to build a recognizable local brand that connects product quality with social impact. As the business develops, we plan to introduce new flavors, strengthen supplier relationships and find more ways to reach customers. Our goal is to create a business that people return to not only for the juice, but also for the values it represents.

FINANCE

Preparing for Launch: Our Initial Startup Cost Research

By Braiden Sottile · Chief Financial Officer

Before The Daily Squeeze can launch, we need a clear understanding of the costs involved in preparing, storing and packaging our juice. Our first stage of financial research focused on three essential purchases: a commercial juicer, a commercial refrigerator and juice bottles.

The commercial juicer will be used to prepare drinks from usable surplus produce. The refrigerator will help keep fruit and prepared juice at appropriate temperatures, while bottles will allow us to package our products for customers. These purchases are important first steps toward creating an efficient and reliable service.

Commercial juicer$802.75
Commercial refrigerator$2,499.00

These three items have a combined estimated cost of $3,449.61 before taxes and shipping. This figure represents only our initial equipment and packaging research, not the full cost of opening the business.

Additional expenses still need to be researched, including transportation, storage containers, cleaning supplies, permits, insurance, marketing and website needs. These costs matter because the business must safely collect produce, prepare drinks and reach customers while managing its resources carefully.

Our next step is to compare suppliers, confirm current prices and develop a more complete startup budget. For potential investors, this research demonstrates our commitment to identifying necessary expenses early and making informed financial decisions before launch.

Original research sources: Standard Restaurant Supply (commercial juicers), Home Depot (commercial refrigerators), and Restaurantware (16 oz juice bottles). Prices are preliminary estimates.

OPERATIONS

Why Orlando Is the Right Place to Build Our Community-Focused Business

By Ruvy Rivera · Chief Operating Officer

The Daily Squeeze plans to operate in Orlando, Florida, where access to fruit and vegetables creates opportunities to source usable produce that might otherwise be discarded. Choosing the right location is important because our business depends on both local suppliers and a community that can support our mission.

Our original research examined Orlando's population, education levels and employment conditions to better understand the local workforce. The research cited a population of 333,888 in July 2025, with 92.2% of residents having a high school education or higher, 42.5% holding a bachelor's degree or higher and an unemployment rate of 4.6%. These figures were used as planning context and should be updated before making final hiring decisions.

Our mission includes creating opportunities for people who need a second chance in the workforce. Understanding the local labor market will help us think about hiring, training and building a supportive team. We also need to balance this goal with realistic staffing costs as a new business.

Our research noted that Florida's minimum wage increased to $15 per hour in September 2026. Labor expenses will therefore be an important part of future operating budgets. As we plan our launch, we will need to determine how many employees are necessary and what responsibilities each role will cover.

For potential investors, our operational priority is to build a manageable business that connects local sourcing with responsible hiring. By carefully planning staffing, labor costs and community relationships, we hope to create a strong foundation for future growth while staying true to our mission.
